3. FIRST-PRINCIPLES DERIVATION
====================================================================

  For unpolarized (incoherent) light, two orthogonal polarization states
  TE and TM are statistically independent and do not interfere.

  The total intensity is the INCOHERENT sum:
    I_unpol = (I_TE + I_TM) / 2

  where I_TE = |E_TE|² and I_TM = |E_TM|² are the intensities of the
  two polarization states.

  For the RCWA solver, the reflected amplitudes are:
    TE: R_m_TE = reflected E_y amplitude / incident E_y amplitude
    TM: R_m_TM = reflected H_y amplitude / incident H_y amplitude

  The correct unpolarized aerial image is:
    I_unpol(x) = (|Σ R_m_TE · exp(i·2π·m·x/Λ)|²
                + |Σ R_m_TM · exp(i·2π·m·x/Λ)|²) / 2

  The pipeline computes:
    I_pipeline(x) = |Σ (R_m_TE + R_m_TM)/2 · exp(i·2π·m·x/Λ)|²

  These are mathematically different:
    I_unpol ≠ I_pipeline

  because the cross term |R_m_TE · R_n_TM*| present in the coherent sum
  is absent in the incoherent sum.

  Test case: if R_TE = A, R_TM = -A for all orders:
    I_unpol = (|A|² + |A|²)/2 = |A|²
    I_pipeline = |(A + (-A))/2|² = 0
    Result: 100% error — artificial cancellation.

  Test case: if R_TE = A, R_TM = B with |A| ≠ |B|:
    I_unpol = (|A|² + |B|²)/2
    I_pipeline = |(A+B)/2|² = (|A|² + |B|² + 2·Re(A·B*))/4
    Error = |I_unpol - I_pipeline|/I_unpol = |(|A|²+|B|²) - 2·Re(A·B*)|/2(|A|²+|B|²)
    For arbitrary complex A, B, this is nonzero unless A = B.

  Additionally, R_m_TE and R_m_TM are physically different quantities:
  E_y field amplitude vs H_y field amplitude. For a plane wave in vacuum,
  they are related by |H_y| = |E_y|/η₀ where η₀ ≈ 377 Ω is the vacuum
  impedance. The incident power normalization differs by a factor of
  η₀² ≈ 142,000 between TE and TM when using unit amplitude excitation.


4. QUESTION A: IS THE MATHEMATICAL TREATMENT OF UNPOLARIZED
   LIGHT CORRECT?
====================================================================

  ANSWER: NO — the field averaging is wrong for unpolarized light.

  Reason: The pipeline averages complex field amplitudes (E_TE + E_TM)/2,
  which is a coherent superposition that creates artificial cross-
  polarization interference. For statistically independent TE and TM
  polarization states, the correct treatment is incoherent intensity
  averaging.

  However, this question is MOOT because the TM solver is buggy
  (see Question B). The averaging issue cannot be correctly addressed
  until the TM solver itself produces physically meaningful results.

  VERDICT: CONFIRMED BUG (secondary to TM solver bug)


5. QUESTION B: IS THE RCWA TE/TM SOLVER ITSELF CORRECT?
====================================================================

  ANSWER: TE solver — YES. TM solver — NO.

  TE solver verification:
  - Matches TMM for uniform slab at normal incidence (all thicknesses)
  - Matches Fresnel equations for bare substrate
  - Energy conservation for lossless gratings (R + T ≈ 1 for TE)
  - Normal incidence symmetry (R(+m) = R(-m))
  - Convergence with increasing Fourier orders

  TM solver failures:
  - At normal incidence, TM ≠ TE for a uniform slab
    (PHYSICAL REQUIREMENT: TE = TM at normal incidence)
  - TM R0 = 0.633 vs TE R0 = 0.001 for 100 nm Ta slab at normal incidence
    (TMM reference: 0.001)
  - TM amplitudes are physically impossible: |R_0|² ≈ 2.1e6 for EUV mask
    (reflectivity > 1 violates passive-structure energy conservation)
  - TM eigenvalue problem uses WRONG first term
    (see Section 11 for mathematical proof)

  ROOT CAUSE: TM eigenvalue problem uses Toeplitz(1/ε) instead of
  Toeplitz(ε) for the first term of the matrix A.

  Code:   A = E - Kx @ inv(E) @ Kx,  where E = Toeplitz(1/ε)
          = Toeplitz(1/ε) - Kx @ inv(Toeplitz(1/ε)) @ Kx

  Correct (Li 1996, Moharam 1995):
          A = Toeplitz(ε) - Kx @ inv(Toeplitz(1/ε)) @ Kx

  VERDICT: TM solver has a P0 bug in the eigenvalue problem formulation


6. QUESTION C: IS THE INTERFACE BETWEEN RCWA AND THE SCALAR
   HOPKINS IMAGING MODEL CORRECT?
====================================================================

  ANSWER: Thin-mask path — YES. RCWA path — NO (because TM is broken).

  Thin-mask path (no RCWA):
  - Uses TMM with te=True (TE polarization only)
  - Amplitudes are complex E-field reflection coefficients
  - These are fed correctly into the scalar Hopkins formula
  - Self-consistent and physically meaningful

  RCWA path:
  - TE amplitudes are E_y reflection coefficients → correct input to Hopkins
  - TM amplitudes are H_y reflection coefficients → WRONG input to Hopkins
    (Hopkins expects E-field amplitudes, not H-field amplitudes)
  - The field-averaged combination is doubly wrong:
    (a) TM amplitudes are in the wrong physical quantity (H_y vs E_y)
    (b) TM amplitudes are numerically wrong (broken solver)
    (c) Field averaging is wrong for incoherent polarization

  VERDICT: BROKEN — due to TM solver bug, not the interface itself

8. ENERGY CONSERVATION ANALYSIS
====================================================================

  The current `diffraction_efficiency()` method returns |R_m|² WITHOUT
  the kz-admittance weighting required for true power.

  Correct diffraction efficiency:
    TE:  DE_m = |R_m|² · Re(kz_m/k0) / Re(kz_0/k0)
    TM:  DE_m = |R_m|² · Re(kz_m/(n²·k0)) / Re(kz_0/(n²·k0))

  For the EUV case (θ=6°, λ=13.5 nm, Λ=64 nm):
    m=0:  kz/k0 = 0.9945,  weighting = 1.000
    m=1:  kz/k0 = 0.9943,  weighting = 0.9998  (negligible)
    m=3:  kz/k0 = 0.8491,  weighting = 0.8538  (15% error)
    m=5:  kz/k0 = 0.3118,  weighting = 0.3135  (68% error)
    m=-5: evanescent,      weighting = 0       (correct)

  The kz weighting is negligible for the first few orders at 6°
  incidence, but becomes significant for high orders. This is a
  separate issue from the TM solver bug and only affects the
  diagnostic `diffraction_efficiency()` method, not the pipeline.

11. MATHEMATICAL PROOF
====================================================================

  The RCWA TM eigenvalue problem in the code (rcwa_torch.py:170-180):

    use_inv = self.cfg.polarization == "TM"           # True for TM
    E = permittivity_toeplitz(eps_profile, ..., use_inverse_rule=use_inv)
      # For TM: E = Toeplitz(1/ε)
    E_inv = torch.linalg.inv(E)
      # E_inv = inv(Toeplitz(1/ε))
    A = E - Kx @ E_inv @ Kx
      # A = Toeplitz(1/ε) - Kx @ inv(Toeplitz(1/ε)) @ Kx

  According to the standard RCWA literature (Moharam 1995, eq. 20;
  Li 1996, eq. 30), the correct TM eigenvalue problem is:

    A = Toeplitz(ε) - Kx @ inv(Toeplitz(1/ε)) @ Kx

  The first term should be the permittivity Toeplitz matrix, NOT the
  inverse-permittivity Toeplitz matrix.

  Physical consequence: At normal incidence for a uniform slab,
  TE and TM must give identical results because the polarization
  states are degenerate. The code's TM solver violates this.

  Note: The exact expression for the "correct" A_TM may depend on
  the specific field component used (H_y vs E_x) and the sign
  convention. The empirical evidence (TM ≠ TE at normal incidence)
  is the definitive proof of a bug. The exact fix requires careful
  literature review and validation.

15. RECOMMENDED NEXT ACTION
====================================================================

  Fix the TM eigenvalue problem in rcwa_torch.py.

  The fix is conceptually:
    For TM polarization, the eigenvalue matrix A should use
    Toeplitz(ε) for the first term, not Toeplitz(1/ε).

  The specific change would be to build TWO Toeplitz matrices:
    E_eps = permittivity_toeplitz(eps_profile, M, use_inverse_rule=False)  # Toeplitz(eps)
    E_inv = permittivity_toeplitz(eps_profile, M, use_inverse_rule=True)   # Toeplitz(1/eps)
    A = E_eps - Kx @ torch.linalg.inv(E_inv) @ Kx

  After fixing the TM solver:
  1. Verify TE = TM at normal incidence (all thicknesses, all materials)
  2. Verify TM matches TMM for uniform slabs
  3. Verify TM energy conservation for lossless gratings
  4. Then fix the polarization averaging (pipeline.py line 676) to
     average intensities, not fields
  5. Add tests that detect TE = TM at normal incidence

  Do NOT attempt to fix the polarization averaging until the TM solver
  is correct — the two issues are distinct and fixing them in the
  wrong order will produce misleading results.

  The thin-mask path (default) is the reliable fallback until the RCWA
  path is fixed. Consider making the RCWA path TE-only as a temporary
  measure, or issue a warning when use_rcwa=True is used.
